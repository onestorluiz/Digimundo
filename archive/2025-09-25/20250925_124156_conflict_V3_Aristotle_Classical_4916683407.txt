**ARISTOTELIAN CONFLICT ANALYSIS OF THE SCRIPT:**

The tragic flaw (**hamartia**) of Marcus, a former priest, is his inability to reconcile his religious teachings with the desire for justice. This internal conflict leads him to play God by poisoning a brain-dead boy's father who confessed to killing his three children (Poetics 1453a).

The dramatic contest (**agon**) in this scene is the philosophical disagreement between Marcus and Elena, both of whom were once religious figures. They represent opposing forces in equilibrium as they debate whether justice or preservation of life should take precedence (Poetics 1450a).

The reversal through conflict (**peripeteia**) occurs when Elena realizes the implication of Marcus's act - that he poisoned the boy's father. Her recognition born from conflict (**anagnorisis**) leads to a change in her understanding of Marcus's actions (Poetics 1452a).

This script adheres to Aristotelian conflict principles:
1. Conflict drives the plot: The moral dilemma facing Marcus propels the narrative forward.
2. Emotional impact through conflict: Fear and pity are evoked as we see Marcus's struggle between his religious beliefs and desire for justice.
3. Unity of action: The script focuses on a single moral dilemma.
4. Probable impossibility in conflict: It is improbable yet possible that a former priest would consider such drastic measures.
5. Catharsis through conflict resolution: There is no clear resolution within this scene, but the potential for catharsis exists beyond it.

The script also utilizes the Classical Unities to intensify the conflict:
- Unity of Time: The entire scene takes place during one night, heightening the tension.
- Unity of Place: The action is confined to Marcus's apartment, concentrating the conflict.
- Unity of Action: The script maintains a singular focus on the moral dilemma.

In conclusion, this script effectively applies Aristotle's concept of dramatic conflict with its exploration of a tragic flaw, philosophical contest, and reversal through recognition. It also adheres to Aristotelian conflict principles and utilizes the Classical Unities to intensify the conflict.
