1. Hitchcock's "Bomb Under the Table" principle: The confession note (the "bomb") is known to the audience before Elena, creating suspense as we anticipate her reaction.
2. Suspense vs Surprise: Hitchcock's MacGuffin driving tension - the confession note serves as a catalyst for escalating tension throughout the scene.
3. McKee's Gap Theory: The audience expects understanding or explanation; however, Marcus confesses to murder, creating a gap between expectation and result that generates tension.
4. Turning Points as Tension Peaks: Each revelation serves as a turning point, increasing suspense through reversals - e.g., the confession itself, Morrison's crime, and Elena's physical touch at the end.
5. McKee's Crisis Decision: Elena is faced with a dilemma between reporting Marcus or protecting him, causing tension due to irreconcilable goods.
6. Progressive Complications Build Tension: The narrative progresses from baseline tension (Marcus alone) to maximum complication (moral debate), followed by a release/rebuild of tension through physical contact.
7. Hitchcock's Visual Tension Techniques: 
   - Objects carry emotional weight: The blood-stained confession note symbolizes guilt and sin.
   - Camera knows more than characters: The audience is aware of Marcus's actions before Elena, enhancing suspense.
   - Silence is loudest sound: The pause after Marcus's confession highlights the gravity of his actions and its impact on Elena.
