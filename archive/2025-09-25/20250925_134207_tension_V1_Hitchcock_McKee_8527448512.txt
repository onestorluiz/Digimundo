1. **Hitchcock's "Bomb Under the Table" Principle**: The confession note is the "bomb" that creates tension through information inequality. We, as the audience, know about it before Elena does, making us aware of a danger she is not.

2. **Suspense vs Surprise**: Hitchcock said surprise lasts 15 seconds while suspense can last 15 minutes. The confession note serves as the MacGuffin driving tension throughout the scene, creating anticipation for how Elena will react when she discovers it.

3. **McKee's Gap Theory**: The gap between expectation and result creates tension. Marcus expects understanding; instead, he gets a moral challenge. Similarly, Elena expects an explanation but receives a murder confession.

4. **Turning Points as Tension Peaks**: Each revelation serves as a turning point, heightening the suspense through reversals. For instance, when Elena learns about the Morrison boy and Marcus's involvement, this is a major turning point that intensifies the tension.

5. **McKee's Crisis Decision**: Elena faces a dilemma - report Marcus or protect him. This creates irreconcilable goods, adding to the overall suspense of the scene.

6. **Progressive Complications Build Tension**: The introduction of complications escalates the tension. Initially, there's baseline tension with just Marcus alone. Then Elena arrives (complication), followed by the revelation of the confession note (major complication), moral debate (maximum complication), and finally, physical touch which provides a momentary release of tension before rebuilding it again.

7. **Hitchcock's Visual Tension Techniques**: Objects carry emotional weight. The blood-stained confession note serves as a constant reminder of the gravity of Marcus's actions. Also, "the camera knows more than characters" principle is applied when we see the note before Elena does. Lastly, "silence is loudest sound" is demonstrated in the pause after Marcus confesses, emphasizing the shock and tension of the moment.

In conclusion, this script masterfully applies Hitchcock's suspense principles and McKee's tension theory techniques to create a highly tense and dramatic scene.
