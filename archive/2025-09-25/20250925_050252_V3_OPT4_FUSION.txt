📚 Part 1: THEORETICAL FRAMEWORK (200 words)

In this script, we apply dialogue theory to understand the deep structure of the conversation between Sarah and Mark. We'll use McKee's Text/Subtext Gap, measuring the distance between said and meant; Mamet's Practical Aesthetics, treating dialogue as action rather than exposition; Sorkin's Musical Rhythm, focusing on cadence, tempo, and verbal jazz; Pinter's Loaded Silence, recognizing that what's not said matters most; Tarantino's Naturalism, allowing for authentic tangents and pop culture references; and Coen Brothers' Specificity, using character-specific vocabulary.

We analyze Voice Architecture by identifying each character's linguistic fingerprint, mapping dialogue patterns to psychological profiles, decoding power dynamics through speech patterns, and analyzing status transactions in conversations. 

Subtext Mechanics involve understanding that each line operates on three levels: surface meaning (what's said), intended effect (what's wanted), and hidden truth (what's revealed). We document how the dialogue succeeds or fails at each level.
