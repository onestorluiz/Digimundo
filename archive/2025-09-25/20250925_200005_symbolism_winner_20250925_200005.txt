1. RED LIGHT BULB: The red light bulb represents danger, passion, and the unconscious mind. It also symbolizes the collective unconscious's "blood" that connects all humanity. The color red is often associated with strong emotions like anger, love, or sacrifice.
2. CRUCIFIX: As a symbol of Christianity, the crucifix represents salvation, sacrifice, and atonement. In this context, it also serves as a reminder of Young Marcus's past experiences in St. Mary's Seminary. The inverted crucifix in Marcus's apartment might indicate that he has turned away from his religious upbringing or is questioning his faith.
3. SYRINGE & VIAL: These items symbolize the shadow archetype, as they are used to "purify" Marcus, representing an invasion of his psyche. The serpent-like needle alludes to the temptation and sin motif found in many religious traditions.
4. MIRROR & INFINITE ROOMS: Mirrors are often Jungian symbols for self-reflection and the subconscious. Here, it reflects infinite red rooms, emphasizing Marcus's internal struggle and the vastness of his unconscious mind.
5. PHOTOS & WHITE SHIRTS: The white shirts symbolize innocence or purity, while the dark stains might represent guilt or corruption. These photos may represent the anima/animus archetype, as Marcus is haunted by these boys' memories and seeks to reconcile with his past.
6. BLACK DOOR: A common Jungian symbol for the unknown or the unconscious, the black door could also be interpreted as a threshold guardian in the hero's journey archetype. It marks a point of no return for Marcus.
7. APPLE: The apple is an allusion to the biblical story of Adam and Eve, representing knowledge, temptation, or sin. Its transformation from fresh fruit to fossilized symbolizes time passing and the transformation of Marcus's psyche.
8. MOTH: In Jungian psychology, moths can represent the human soul seeking enlightenment. The first moth burning in the red bulb could symbolize the suffering or sacrifice necessary for growth, while the second moth landing on the gun unharmed suggests that Marcus has found some resolution or acceptance regarding his past and is ready to face whatever lies ahead.
9. GUN & BULLETS: These items represent action and determination. Six bullets for six names might indicate a sense of closure or justice that Marcus seeks. The bullets' resemblance to communion wafers may be a subtle reference to the sacrament, tying back to the religious motif.
10. MILITARY JACKET: This symbolizes authority, protection, and perhaps aggression. By wearing his father's military jacket, Marcus might feel empowered or prepared for what lies ahead, embracing a more assertive role in his hero's journey.
11. ROSARY & BEADS: Rosaries are typically associated with prayer and devotion in the Catholic faith. The beads resembling drops of blood frozen in time can represent both the suffering experienced by Marcus and his mother and their deep-rooted faith.

Overall, this script explores various Jungian archetypes and symbols, including the collective unconscious, shadow, anima/animus, mandala, and hero's journey. It delves into themes of sacrifice, guilt, redemption, and self-discovery while incorporating religious motifs that add depth to Marcus's character development and personal growth.
