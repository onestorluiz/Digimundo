1. **FEAR AND PITY AS TENSION SOURCES:** Fear is evoked through Marcus's spiritual jeopardy, his fear of divine punishment for taking a life, and the fear of discovery and consequences by Elena. Pity arises from understanding Marcus's moral struggle in dealing with the trauma of child murders, Elena's impossible position as a former nun confronted with this situation, and the misfortune of the murdered children.

2. **DRAMATIC IRONY CREATING TENSION:** Dramatic irony is created by the audience knowing Marcus's actions before Elena does, which generates curiosity about how she will react upon discovering the truth.

3. **RECOGNITION (ANAGNORISIS) AS TENSION PEAK:** The tension reaches its peak when Elena recognizes Marcus's heinous act and his self-realization of sinning, symbolized by his line "I have sinned."

4. **REVERSAL (PERIPETEIA) THROUGH TENSION:** A reversal occurs as Marcus seeks comfort from Elena but instead receives judgment. Elena, initially coming to help, assumes the role of a moral judge further intensifying the emotional conflict.

5. **HAMARTIA DRIVING TENSION:** Marcus's tragic flaw (hamartia) of playing God by taking justice into his own hands creates internal and external tension.

6. **UNITY OF TIME INTENSIFYING TENSION:** The single-night setting and real-time conversation heighten the emotional impact as the characters grapple with their choices.

7. **CATHARSIS THROUGH TENSION RELEASE:** There is a partial catharsis, or release of emotion, when Elena empathizes with Marcus's struggle despite his terrible act, reminding us that even those who have fallen from grace can inspire compassion within us. However, the tension remains unresolved, fitting for modern tragedy where complex moral dilemmas often remain ambiguous. 

In conclusion, Aristotelian principles of fear, pity, and dramatic irony create a gripping narrative in this script that explores heavy themes of morality, faith, justice, and their consequences.
