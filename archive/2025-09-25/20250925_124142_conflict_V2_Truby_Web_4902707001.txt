1. **TRUBY'S FOUR-CORNER OPPOSITION**

Truby states, "Great stories don't have one opponent but a web of opposition" (Anatomy of Story, p87). In this script, we can analyze the four-corner opposition as follows:

   - **Hero vs Main Opponent**: Marcus (the former priest) and Elena (the nurse, his ally) versus the moral implications of Marcus's actions. The main conflict lies in their differing values regarding justice and forgiveness.
   
   - **Hero vs First Ally/Opponent**: Marcus vs Elena. While they were once part of the same faith, Marcus's actions challenge Elena's beliefs, creating internal conflict within their relationship.
   
   - **Hero vs Second Ally/Opponent**: Marcus vs the implied authority figures (the Church or Law enforcement). Although not present in the script, their potential disapproval and consequences add tension.
   
   - **All three opponents vs each other**: The moral implications of Marcus's actions, Elena's faith, and the external authority figures create a complex web of opposition that drives the narrative forward.

2. **TRUBY'S CONFLICT THROUGH VALUES**

Truby states, "moral argument is the heart of conflict" (Anatomy of Story, p112). Here, Marcus and Elena embody different value systems:

   - Marcus believes in taking justice into his own hands for those who cannot receive it otherwise.
   - Elena values the preservation of life above all else and sees Marcus's actions as a violation of their shared oath.

3. **TRUBY'S 22-STEP CONFLICT ESCALATION**

   - Step 1: Weakness/Need (internal conflict seed): Marcus struggles with his faith, questioning the justice system and grappling with his own sense of morality.
   
   - Step 5: First Opponent appearance: Elena enters as an ally but also presents a moral challenge to Marcus's actions.
   
   - Step 8: Plan creates conflict expectation: Marcus has made a plan to administer justice, which is now in direct opposition to Elena's beliefs and the implied external authorities.
   
   - Step 13: Battle is conflict climax: Although not depicted in this script, the moral battle between Marcus, Elena, and the external authorities would be the climax of the story.
   
   - Step 21: New equilibrium after conflict: The outcome of their moral struggle would determine a new equilibrium for Marcus and Elena, whether reconciled or further estranged.

4. **TRUBY'S UNIQUE TECHNIQUES**

   - Conflict through competing philosophies (page 134): Marcus and Elena have fundamentally different beliefs about justice and forgiveness.
   
   - The opponent is the hero's double (page 91): Elena, once a nun like Marcus, serves as an embodiment of his former self and challenges him on his new path.
   
   - Best opponent attacks hero's greatest weakness (page 93): By questioning his actions and reminding him of their shared oath, Elena taps into Marcus's inner conflict surrounding justice and faith.
   
   - Conflict reveals character (page 156): The conversation between Marcus and Elena sheds light on their individual beliefs and how they handle moral dilemmas.
   
   - Moral decisions under pressure (page 167): Faced with the aftermath of Marcus's actions, Elena urges him to consider the consequences and not lose his soul.
