1. FEAR AND PITY AS TENSION SOURCES:
	* Fear for Marcus's soul due to spiritual jeopardy and divine punishment.
	* Pity for Marcus's moral struggle, the murdered children, and Elena's impossible position as a former nun.
2. DRAMATIC IRONY CREATING TENSION:
	* The audience knows Marcus is a murderer before Elena does, creating tension in the scene.
3. RECOGNITION (ANAGNORISIS) AS TENSION PEAK:
	* Elena's recognition of Marcus's act and Marcus's self-recognition as a sinner intensify the emotional impact of the story.
4. REVERSAL (PERIPETEIA) THROUGH TENSION:
	* Marcus seeks comfort but receives judgment from Elena, who becomes the moral judge in this situation.
5. HAMARTIA DRIVING TENSION:
	* Marcus's tragic flaw of "playing God" creates internal and external tension within the narrative.
6. UNITY OF TIME INTENSIFYING TENSION:
	* The single-night setting in real-time increases the emotional impact, creating a sense of urgency and claustrophobia.
7. CATHARSIS THROUGH TENSION RELEASE:
	* A partial catharsis is achieved through the release of tension, although some unresolved tension remains, making it relatable to modern tragedies.

Quote Aristotle: "Plot is soul of tragedy" (1450a38) - and tension is the soul of plot in this script. The application of Aristotelian principles effectively creates a gripping narrative that evokes fear, pity, and empathy from the audience through dramatic irony, recognition, reversal, hamartia, unity of time, and catharsis.
