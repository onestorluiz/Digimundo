1. Central Dramatic Question: The central dramatic question of this script is whether Marcus will lose his soul for seeking justice. This question sets up tension as the audience wonders about the moral implications of Marcus's actions and how it will affect him spiritually.

2. Desire vs Obstacle: Marcus wants understanding and absolution, while Elena's moral stance serves as an obstacle. Tension arises from their conflicting perspectives, as Marcus struggles with guilt and Elena tries to uphold her beliefs.

3. Revelation Sequence Creating Tension: The script has several key revelations that build tension:
   - First revelation: Marcus killed someone (implied through the confession note)
   - Second revelation: It was the Morrison boy, which intensifies the stakes as the victim is a child
   - Third revelation: The method of murder using communion wine, highlighting the moral complexity and blurring lines between religious rituals and justice

4. Opposition Escalation: The opposition escalates in several ways, creating exponential tension:
   - Level 1: Marcus vs himself (guilt)
   - Level 2: Marcus vs Elena (moral opposition)
   - Level 3: Marcus vs divine law (playing God by taking a life and administering justice)
   - Level 4: Marcus vs human law (committing murder)

5. Moral Dilemma as Tension Source: The script explores the moral argument between justice and law, divine authority versus human authority, and mercy versus punishment, which drives tension throughout the story.

6. Subplot Weaving for Tension: The lost faith history subplot contrasts with Marcus's confession plot, creating tension by highlighting their shared experiences of losing faith but diverging paths when it comes to morality.

7. Desire Line Maintaining Tension: Both Marcus and Elena have conflicting desire lines – Marcus desires understanding, absolution, and peace while Elena wants to help her friend, uphold morality, and save Marcus's soul. Their contrasting desires create sustained tension as they struggle to reconcile their beliefs and actions.

In conclusion, this script effectively uses Truby's dramatic question and revelation techniques to create tension and engage the audience in a moral exploration of justice, faith, and guilt.
