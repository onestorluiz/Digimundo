1. Interpretation

The Python analysis reveals that the screenplay has several areas that need improvement, particularly in terms of character development and dialogue. According to Seger's theory, "the real character is revealed under pressure" (Seger, p. 17), which suggests that Samantha's true personality is not fully developed yet. The overall quality of the screenplay can be improved by focusing on creating more complex characters with distinct personalities and motivations. Additionally, the dialogue needs to be revised to avoid being too expository or "on-the-nose," as recommended by Seger (p. 29).
2. Patterns

The recurring pattern of evasion of reality by Samantha is evident throughout the screenplay. This behavior is first observed when she chooses to focus on her dream instead of facing the present after waking up. As Seger explains, "characters who explain their feelings instead of acting are less interesting than those who show us what they feel through their actions" (p. 29). Therefore, it is essential to revise the dialogue and replace it with actions that demonstrate Samantha's emotions and thoughts. Furthermore, the use of flashbacks and nonlinear narrative structure adds complexity to the story but may also create confusion if not handled carefully.
3. Problems

Problem 1: Lack of Character Development
Samantha's character lacks depth and complexity, making it difficult for the audience to connect with her emotionally. According to Seger, "characters must have goals, motivation, and conflict to make them compelling" (p. 17). Currently, Samantha's goal is unclear, and her motivation is limited to escaping reality. To address this issue, the writer should develop Samantha's backstory and provide clearer goals and motivations.
Impact: A poorly developed character can lead to disinterest and disengagement from the audience.
Problem 2: On-the-Nose Dialogue
As mentioned earlier, the dialogue in the screenplay is often too expository and does not reveal the characters' inner thoughts and feelings. Seger advises against using dialogue that states the obvious because it can come across as artificial and forced (p. 29). Instead, the writer should focus on showing the characters' emotions through their actions and body language.
Impact: On-the-nose dialogue can make the characters seem flat and uninteresting, reducing the emotional impact of the story.
Problem 3: Nonlinear Narrative Structure
While the use of a nonlinear narrative structure can add complexity and intrigue to the story, it can also create confusion if not executed properly. The writer should ensure that each scene transitions smoothly into the next and provides enough context for the audience to follow along.
Impact: Confusing narrative structure can distract the audience from the story and reduce its emotional impact.
4. Solutions

Solution 1: Develop Samantha's Character
To address the lack of character development, the writer should explore Samantha's backstory and provide clearer goals and motivations. For example, the writer could introduce a conflict that forces Samantha to confront her fears and take action. This would give Samantha a clear objective and create tension in the story.
Grounded in Theory: Seger emphasizes the importance of having well-developed characters with clear goals, motivations, and conflicts (p. 17).
Example: In the opening scene, Samantha could be shown struggling with anxiety or depression, providing insight into her inner world and setting up a clear goal for her character.
Expected Outcome: A well-developed character will engage the audience emotionally and create a stronger connection between the viewer and the protagonist.
Solution 2: Revise the Dialogue
To avoid on-the-nose dialogue, the writer should focus on showing the characters' emotions through their actions and body language rather than stating them explicitly. For instance, instead of having Samantha say, "I'm scared," the writer could show her trembling hands or avoiding eye contact.
Grounded in Theory: Seger recommends using subtext and indirect communication to create more natural and engaging dialogue (p. 29).
Example: When Alberto asks Samantha how she feels, she could respond by saying, "Can we talk about something else?" instead of directly expressing her fear.
Expected Outcome: More nuanced dialogue will make the characters seem more authentic and relatable, enhancing the emotional impact of the story.
Solution 3: Clarify the Narrative Structure
To prevent confusion caused by the nonlinear narrative structure, the writer should ensure that each scene transitions smoothly into the next and provides enough context for the audience to follow along. For example, the writer could use visual cues or sound effects to indicate changes in time and place.
Grounded in Theory: Seger stresses the importance of maintaining clarity and coherence in the narrative structure (p. 58).
Example: Before transitioning to a flashback, the writer could include a visual cue such as a fade to black or white to signal the change in time.
Expected Outcome: Clearer narrative structure will help the audience stay engaged and focused on the story.
5. Depth & Synthesis

By addressing these problems, the writer can create a more engaging and emotionally resonant screenplay. Interconnecting these issues will result in a deeper understanding of the characters and their struggles. Additionally, focusing on creating complex characters, natural dialogue, and clear narrative structure will enhance the overall quality of the screenplay.
Final Professional Assessment: While the screenplay has potential, it requires further development to create a compelling and emotionally engaging story. By following Seger's theory and addressing the identified problems, the writer can elevate the screenplay to a professional level.
