a. Overreliance on expository dialogue: Characters frequently explain their thoughts and feelings directly, which undermines the potential impact of subtext and emotional moments. This issue stems from a misunderstanding of Cowgill's concept of "on-the-nose dialogue," where characters express emotions instead of demonstrating them through actions or indirect language. In the screenplay, however, characters often blend together due to insufficient background information and inconsistent behavior.
c. Disjointed narrative structure: The story jumps erratically between time periods without clear transitions or purpose, causing confusion and disrupting the flow. Cowgill stresses the significance of establishing a coherent timeline and logical progression to maintain audience engagement. d. Lack of tension and conflict: The story unfolds without significant obstacles or challenges for the protagonist, diminishing the sense of urgency and limiting opportunities for character growth. Drawing upon Cowgill's teachings, conflicts are essential for driving narratives forward and revealing characters' true selves. For instance, introducing a tangible antagonist or raising the stakes could enhance the overall story and make it more captivating.
4. Solutions

a. Reduce expository dialogue by incorporating subtext and action: Encourage characters to communicate implicitly, allowing the audience to infer their intentions and emotions based on context clues. For example, replace Samantha's direct admission of having a nice dream with subtle physical gestures or ambiguous statements that hint at her feelings.
b. Strengthen character development through backstory and consistency: Provide each character with a unique voice, history, and goals, ensuring that their decisions and behaviors remain aligned throughout the story. Implementing flashbacks or inner monologues can offer valuable insights into characters' psyches, fostering deeper connections with the audience.
c. Establish a clear and linear narrative structure: Organize scenes chronologically, using transitional devices to guide the audience through shifts in time or location. Consider rearranging certain sequences to improve pacing and clarify any confusing elements identified during the Python analysis.
d. Introduce meaningful conflicts and raise the stakes: Develop an antagonist or external force that poses a legitimate threat to the protagonist's objectives, pushing them to confront their fears, weaknesses, or limitations. Doing so will generate suspense, heighten dramatic tension, and provide ample opportunities for character growth.
