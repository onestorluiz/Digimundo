Description: Characters frequently declare their thoughts and feelings explicitly, rather than conveying them through action or subtext.
Example: In scene 7, a supporting character blurts out her romantic interest in the protagonist instead of demonstrating her attraction through subtle behaviors.
Impact: On-the-nose dialogue can feel artificial and unrealistic, diminishing the emotional resonance of a scene.
Theory Connection: McKee advises writers to avoid direct expressions of emotion, suggesting that "subtextual dialogue carries greater weight than literal speech" (McKee, p. 92).
Problem 3: Inconsistent Theme Development
Description: The central theme of the screenplay is not consistently developed throughout the narrative, resulting in a fragmented storyline.
Example: The theme of self-discovery is introduced in scene 1 but then largely absent until the climax.
Impact: An inconsistent theme weakens the overall coherence of the story, leaving audiences uncertain about its message or purpose.
Theory Connection: McKee stresses the importance of integrating theme into every aspect of the narrative, asserting that "theme is the ultimate source of story power" (McKee, p. 178).
4. Solutions

Solution 1: Strengthen Character Motivation
Action: Review each character's arc and ensure that their decisions stem from clear, compelling motivations.
Grounded Theory: McKee notes that "a motivated character is inherently dynamic" (McKee, p. 123), so building solid foundations for each character will create more engaging storylines.
Implementation Example: In scene 3, provide the protagonist with a specific reason for confronting her boss, such as learning about a hidden project that affects her job security.
Expected Outcome: Stronger character motivation will result in more relatable and immersive storytelling.
Solution 2: Rewrite On-the-Nose Dialogue
Action: Replace explicit declarations of emotion with subtle cues and subtextual language.
Grounded Theory: McKee suggests that "dialogue rich in subtext creates an air of mystery around characters" (McKee, p. 92), encouraging audiences to invest in their journeys.
Implementation Example: In scene 7, have the supporting character reveal her romantic interest through body language, tone of voice, or carefully chosen words, rather than directly confessing her feelings.

Solution 3: Enhance Theme Integration
Action: Identify opportunities to reinforce the central theme throughout the narrative, particularly during key turning points.
Grounded Theory: According to McKee, "story events must dramatize the theme" (McKee, p. 178) to maximize its impact on the audience.
Implementation Example: Incorporate elements of self-discovery into scene 5, when the protagonist faces a significant challenge at work, highlighting her growth and development.
Expected Outcome: Consistent theme integration will give the screenplay a stronger sense of purpose and direction, enhancing its overall appeal.
