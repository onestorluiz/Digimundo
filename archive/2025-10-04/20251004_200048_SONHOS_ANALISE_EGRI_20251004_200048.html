Solution 1: Strengthening Characters
Specific Action: Develop detailed backstories for each major character, highlighting their strengths, weaknesses, desires, and fears. Utilize subtext and body language to convey underlying emotions and motivations.

Example: Flesh out the protagonist's background, explaining why they behave the way they do and how their past experiences shape their present actions. Incorporate subtle gestures and facial expressions to reveal hidden emotions.
Expected Outcome: Stronger, more dynamic characters that resonate with audiences and drive the narrative forward.
Solution 2: Improving Dialogue
Specific Action: Study examples of effective dialogue from films and literature, paying close attention to how authors use word choice, rhythm, and structure to create realistic conversations. Revise existing dialogue to eliminate cliches and introduce more authentic, natural-sounding exchanges between characters.
Grounded in Theory: Egri argues that dialogue should serve multiple functions, including revealing character, advancing the plot, and contributing to the overall mood and atmosphere of the story.
Example: Replace stilted lines with more conversational alternatives, incorporating pauses, interruptions, and imperfections to mimic real-life conversation. Use descriptive language and vivid imagery to bring scenes to life.
Expected Outcome: More engaging, believable dialogue that enhances the audience's connection to the characters and the story.
Solution 3: Balancing Pacing
Specific Action: Review the screenplay to identify areas where pacing could be improved, either by condensing lengthy scenes or expanding upon brief ones. Ensure that each scene contributes to the overall narrative arc and maintains the audience's interest.
