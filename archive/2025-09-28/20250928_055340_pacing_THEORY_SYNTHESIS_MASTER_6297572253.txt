	CLASSICAL FOUNDATIONS (200 words)

Aristotle's principle of unity of time and dramatic progression is evident in this script. The story unfolds within a condensed period, heightening tension and suspense. The narrative follows his dictum, "A whole is that which has beginning, middle and end," with the beginning establishing Sarah's mundane routine, the middle introducing conflict through mysterious phone calls and explosions, and the end revealing kidnappers escaping with the CEO.

Field's Three-Act Structure also applies here. Act One (Setup) shows Sarah at her desk, setting up the corporate environment. The Confrontation starts when Sarah receives the distorted call, escalating with each explosion. Resolution occurs as Sarah calls 911, signifying a shift from chaos to action.

McKee's scene-by-scene pacing progression is demonstrated through rising dramatic pressure. Each scene builds on the previous one, intensifying the stakes and revealing character traits. For instance, Sarah's calm response to initial confusion showcases her resilience under stress.

MODERN APPLICATIONS (200 words)

Truby's Moral Argument comes alive through pacing that reveals character. Sarah's reaction to the crisis exposes her determination and courage, setting up potential growth as she navigates this unexpected situation. The tempo serves to highlight these qualities, showing rather than telling the audience about Sarah's character.

Vogler's Hero's Journey stages are mapped onto the script's pacing beats. The Ordinary World is established in the opening scene; Call to Adventure comes via the distorted phone call; Refusal of the Call is seen in Sarah's initial disbelief and hesitation; Crossing the Threshold happens when she picks up the phone to call 911, marking her entry into the Special World.

Save the Cat's beat sheet principles are reflected in the pacing. Each beat serves a purpose, advancing the story. For example, the Break Into Two occurs at the second explosion, dividing the script into before and after the major incident.

INTEGRATED DIAGNOSIS (200 words)

Different frameworks complement each other in diagnosing the pacing of this script. Classical theories like Aristotle's provide a foundation for understanding story structure, while modern applications offer nuanced insights into character development and emotional arcs.

McKee and Vogler provide complementary perspectives on pacing and structure. McKee focuses on the overall narrative flow, while Vogler brings attention to the hero's emotional journey within that framework. Combining these theories can offer specific solutions, such as ensuring each stage of the Hero's Journey matches the intended dramatic progression.
