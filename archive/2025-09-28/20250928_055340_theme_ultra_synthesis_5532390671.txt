**I. ARISTOTLE'S POETICS**

Aristotle believed that tragedy serves a moral purpose and the theme is essential for catharsis and audience enlightenment. In this script, the theme of morality versus justice unfolds through the characters' struggles with their faith and past vocations. For instance, Marcus questions whether it was just to help a remorseless killer die, while Elena urges him not to lose his soul by taking matters into his own hands.

Quote: "The poet's job is not to tell people how to be good but to reveal the consequences of being bad." (Aristotle's Poetics)

**II. ROBERT MCKEE'S STORY**

McKee emphasizes that theme is meaning and must be earned through story, expressed through action rather than dialogue. The script illustrates this in Marcus' struggle with his own moral values and the conflict between preserving life and seeking justice for innocent victims. This inner turmoil drives Marcus to take drastic measures, ultimately leading to the exploration of themes like guilt, redemption, and faith.

Quote: "Stories are metaphors for life; they explore fundamental aspects of human experience." (Robert McKee, Story)

**III. JOHN TRUBY'S ANATOMY OF STORY**

Truby argues that moral argument is the backbone of a story and theme should be woven throughout character arcs and plot. In this script, both Marcus and Elena grapple with their own value systems, which leads to an exploration of themes related to faith, morality, and justice. The opposition of values (preserving life vs. seeking justice) creates thematic tension that drives the narrative forward.

Quote: "Theme is not a message but a complex argument or exploration." (John Truby, Anatomy of Story)

**IV. SYD FIELD'S SCREENPLAY**

According to Field, theme should be established in the setup and explored throughout the three-act structure. In this script, we see the theme of morality versus justice unfold through Marcus and Elena's discussions about their past lives as religious figures and their current roles in society. Their character growth serves a thematic purpose, reinforcing the idea that faith alone cannot provide true justice.

Quote: "Theme is the underlying message or truth of your story." (Syd Field, Screenplay)

**V. CHRISTOPHER VOGLER'S WRITER'S JOURNEY**

Vogler suggests that universal themes are essential in monomyth structure and theme serves the hero's transformation. In this script, Marcus and Elena represent heroes who must confront their loss of faith and struggle to find a balance between morality and justice. Through their trials and growth, they learn valuable lessons about spirituality, forgiveness, and redemption.

Quote: "The Hero's Journey is an archetypal pattern that can be seen in stories from around the world." (Christopher Vogler, Writer's Journey)

**VI. BLAKE SNYDER'S SAVE THE CAT**

Snyder insists that theme should be stated early and explored throughout each story beat. In this script, the theme of morality versus justice is evident in Marcus' initial decision to assist the killer and Elena's efforts to prevent him from losing his soul. The thematic resolution occurs when Marcus recognizes the error of his ways and embraces redemption instead of vengeance.

Quote: "Genre tells us what kind of story we're telling." (Blake Snyder, Save the Cat)

Campbell posits that universal human themes are present in mythologies across cultures and that theme represents the collective unconscious expression of spiritual transformation. In this script, Marcus and Elena embody these archetypal themes as they confront their loss of faith, struggle with moral quandaries, and ultimately undergo a form of spiritual transformation.

Quote: "The hero's journey is a pattern found in stories from cultures throughout history." (Joseph Campbell, Hero with a Thousand Faces)
