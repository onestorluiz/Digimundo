We begin with ARISTOTLE's POETICS. The fear and pity aroused in the audience come from Marcus's act of giving the Morrison boy communion wine laced with poison, a sin that leads to moral dilemma and catharsis. The suspense is heightened by dramatic irony; we know what Marcus has done, but Elena does not, creating tension in her discovery and reaction. The unity of time intensifies the tension as the entire story unfolds in one night, increasing the claustrophobic atmosphere and the urgency of their moral struggle.

ROBERT MCKEE's STORY teaches us that desire meeting obstacle creates tension. Marcus desires justice for the dead children and faces obstacles within himself - his faith, his vows, and his soul. The gap between expectation (forgiveness through confession) and result (death by poison) builds tension, while turning points like Elena's arrival release and rebuild tension. Subtext also plays a role; under their dialogue lies the unspoken question of whether Marcus can ever be forgiven or find redemption.

In JOHN TRUBY's ANATOMY OF STORY, the dramatic question is "Will Marcus find justice or succumb to his guilt?" His desire line maintains narrative tension as he grapples with his choices and their consequences. Reveals and reversals, such as learning about the Morrison boy being brain dead, increase the stakes and deepen the tension. Opponent escalation is evident in Elena's attempts to dissuade Marcus from his path, creating internal tension.

SYD FIELD's SCREENPLAY provides a structural perspective on tension building. The plot points (Marcus poisoning the boy and Elena discovering the truth) act as pivots that shift tension dynamics. Act structure plays a significant role in managing page-by-page tension, with the climax being the moment when Marcus must confront his actions and their implications.

CHRISTOPHER VOGLER's WRITER'S JOURNEY outlines a journey of escalating tension. Crossing thresholds (from priest to poisoner) raises the stakes, tests create tension (Elena challenging Marcus's choices), and approaching the inmost cave (his own guilt and soul) builds dread. The ordeal is the moment when Marcus must accept responsibility for his actions, leading to a death and rebirth pattern as he decides whether to continue down this path.

BLAKE SNYDER's SAVE THE CAT contributes beat-level tension analysis. The promise of the premise creates expectations (a priest seeking justice), while the B-story (Elena dealing with her past) provides tension relief. Fun and Games establishes a tension baseline, the midpoint raises the stakes (realization of what Marcus has done), and bad guys close in escalation (Marcus's inner turmoil).

Finally, JOSEPH CAMPBELL's HERO WITH A THOUSAND FACES adds mythic tension. Entering the belly of the whale (embracing his darker side) and undergoing a road of trials (grappling with guilt and justice) create an epic sense of struggle. Meeting with goddess/temptress could be Elena, who embodies both compassion and temptation for Marcus to follow or abandon his path.

CONTRADICTIONS AND SYNTHESIS:
While each theorist provides valuable insights into narrative tension, they sometimes contradict each other. For instance, McKee's "tension is created by desire meeting obstacle" contrasts with Vogler's focus on external tests and trials creating tension. To reconcile these differences, we must understand that both internal and external factors contribute to tension.

Internal desires clashing with external obstacles create a complex web of narrative tension that can be analyzed using all seven approaches. By synthesizing these theories, we gain a comprehensive understanding of the intricate layers of tension within this script.
