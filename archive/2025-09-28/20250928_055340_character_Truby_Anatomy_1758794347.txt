1. WEAKNESS AND NEED:

Marcus' psychological weakness is his inability to forgive himself, which manifests as a lack of self-worth and an inclination towards self-destructive behavior (drinking). His moral weakness is his tendency to take justice into his own hands, causing harm to others in the process. Marcus needs to learn that forgiveness, both from others and himself, is crucial for personal growth and redemption.

Elena's psychological weakness is her guilt over past events, leading her to question her decisions and actions. Her moral weakness is her detachment and loss of faith in the sanctity of human life. Elena needs to learn that she cannot control the outcome of every situation and must find a way to reconcile with her past actions.

2. DESIRE LINE:

Marcus' external goal is to atone for his sins by confessing to Elena and seeking redemption. The spine of action includes Marcus recounting the events that led him to this moment, revealing his internal struggle between good and evil. Obstacles include Elena's resistance to hearing his confession due to her own guilt and their shared past experiences.

Elena's external goal is to understand Marcus' actions and confront her own feelings of guilt. The spine of action includes her reactions to Marcus' confession, challenging her beliefs about life and death. Obstacles include her reluctance to revisit the past events that led to her losing faith in herself and others.

3. GHOST AND BACKSTORY:

Marcus' ghost is the memory of Elena allowing the Morrison boy to die, which has haunted him and influenced his recent actions. The backstory reveals how this event impacted Marcus, leading him down a darker path. We learn about it through Marcus' confession to Elena, who is an integral part of his past.

Elena's ghost is the Morrison boy's death and her role in it, which still haunts her and impacts her decisions. The backstory reveals how she became disillusioned with her faith and turned away from her priestly duties, leading her to question the value of human life.

4. SELF-REVELATION:

Marcus' false beliefs include the idea that he can right the wrongs of others through his actions, even if it means crossing ethical boundaries. The battle is Marcus confronting his guilt and seeking forgiveness, leading him to question whether his actions were justified. The revelation is the realization that redemption comes from within, not from external actions.

Elena's false beliefs include her belief that she cannot save everyone and has no control over life and death. The battle is Elena confronting her guilt and finding a way to reconcile with her past actions. The revelation is the realization that forgiveness, both for herself and others, is crucial for personal growth and redemption.

Truby principle: "The self-revelation is the emotional discovery the hero makes about himself or the world in act two that allows him to solve his problem in act three." Marcus' self-revelation about forgiveness and Elena's self-revelation about forgiveness will enable them to move forward and find closure.
