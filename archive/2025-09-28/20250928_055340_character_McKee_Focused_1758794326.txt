1. CHARACTER vs CHARACTERIZATION

Marcus is characterized as a priest, with his loose collar and confessional demeanor. However, his true character is revealed under pressure when he admits to giving communion wine laced with poison to a murderer, showing his willingness to take justice into his own hands. The gap between Marcus' mask and truth is vast; he's not the holy figure he appears to be but a man pushed to desperate measures.

Elena is characterized as a pragmatic ex-priest, hardened by her experiences. Her true character emerges when she confronts Marcus about his actions, displaying shock and disapproval at his decision to poison the confessed killer. The distance between Elena's tough exterior and her moral compass reveals a complex individual.

2. CONSCIOUS vs UNCONSCIOUS DESIRE

Marcus consciously desires redemption for what he perceives as a failure, confessing his sins to Elena. Subconsciously, however, he needs closure and justification for his actions, which leads him to poison the murderer. This contradiction shows his inner turmoil and humanity.

Elena consciously wants to help Marcus, sitting beside him despite her initial reluctance. Unconsciously, she needs to confront her own past mistakes, specifically the death of the Morrison boy, which mirrors Marcus' situation. 

3. DIMENSIONAL ANALYSIS

Marcus is internally contradictory: a man of God who resorts to murder; a protector turned executioner. His actions align with his nature as a desperate individual seeking justice. McKee's dimensional scale would rate Marcus high due to these compelling contradictions and complexities.

Elena also exhibits internal contradictions, such as her faith in the face of tragedy and her role as a healer grappling with decisions about who deserves saving. Her actions align with her nature as a woman haunted by her past yet still committed to helping others. She too ranks high on McKee's dimensional scale.

4. PRESSURE COOKER MOMENTS

1. When Marcus confesses to Elena that he poisoned the murderer, pressuring her to respond. He chose to reveal his actions, while she could have chosen to walk away but didn't.

2. When Elena realizes the implication of Marcus' words - that he gave communion wine mixed with poison to the murderer. This moment tests her faith and moral compass, pressuring her to confront her own past mistakes.

3. The climax occurs when Marcus reveals the blood-stained confession note, confirming his actions. This instance forces Elena (and the audience) to grapple with the consequences of their choices and beliefs.

These pressure cooker moments reveal characters' true natures through their actions, aligning with McKee's principle that "Pressure is essential. Choices made when nothing is at risk mean little."
