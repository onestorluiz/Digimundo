1. INT. MARCUS'S APARTMENT - NIGHT

   **THOUGHT THROUGH SPEECH (Aristotle, Poetics 1450b)**: Marcus speaks his internal conflict aloud, revealing his struggle between his past religious beliefs and current moral dilemma.
   
   MARCUS (42), a former priest, sits alone with a bottle of whiskey. His clerical collar hangs loose around his neck.

   On the table: a blood-stained confession note.

   **EXPOSITION THROUGH CONFLICT (Field, Screenplay 215)**: The conflict between Marcus and his past as a priest is introduced, revealing necessary information about his character through argument.
   
   MARCUS
   (to himself)
   Forgive me, Father, for I have sinned.

   **SUBTEXT (McKee, Story 389-392)**: Marcus's statement is not a direct conversation with God but an expression of his guilt and inner turmoil.
   
   He pours another drink. His hands shake.
   
   The door opens. ELENA (38) enters, leather jacket over scrubs. She was a nun once - now works as a nurse.
   
   **MORAL DIALOGUE (Truby, Anatomy of Story 418)**: The dialogue between Marcus and Elena reveals their contrasting philosophies and moral values.
   
   ELENA
   You called me.

   MARCUS
   I needed... someone who'd understand.

   **DIALOGUE AS ACTION (McKee, Story 393)**: Marcus's dialogue changes the relationship between him and Elena by admitting his need for understanding.
   
   ELENA
   (sitting across from him)
   What did you do, Marcus?

   Marcus slides the confession note across the table. Elena reads it, her face growing pale.

   ELENA (CONT'D)
   The Morrison boy... he was brain dead.

   MARCUS
   His father confessed. Three children.
   Beat them to death with a baseball bat.

   **SILENT DIALOGUE (McKee, Story 400)**: Elena's silence communicates her shock and disgust, revealing her character without words.
   
   ELENA
   So you...

   MARCUS
   Communion wine. With something extra.

   **THOUGHT THROUGH SPEECH (Aristotle, Poetics 1450b)**: Marcus's statement reveals his inner moral justification for his actions.
   
   Silence. Elena stares at the note.

   ELENA
   We swore an oath to preserve life.

   MARCUS
   And what about justice? What about those children?

   **RECOGNITION THROUGH WORDS (Aristotle, Poetics 1452b)**: Elena begins to understand the depth of Marcus's moral crisis and his desire for justice.
   
   ELENA
   That wasn't your choice to make.

   MARCUS
   (standing, agitated)
   Wasn't it? He came to me for absolution.
   Told me everything. Bragged about it.

   **MORAL DIALOGUE (Truby, Anatomy of Story 418)**: Marcus continues to express his moral dilemma as Elena listens and responds with her own beliefs.
   
   ELENA
   You're not God, Marcus.

   MARCUS
   No. But sometimes God needs help.

   **DIALOGUE AS AMmunITION (McKee, Story 395)**: Elena uses her dialogue as a weapon to challenge Marcus's moral stance.
   
   Elena reaches across, takes his hand.

   ELENA
   We both lost our faith once. Don't lose your soul too.

   **DIALOGUE HOOK (Field, Screenplay 222)**: Elena's final statement leaves a memorable line that resonates and creates curiosity for the audience.
